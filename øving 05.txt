3. What does the SIM acronym mean?

4. What does “tamper resistant” mean in practice?
    Tamper resistance means making it more difficult to tamper with (gain unauthorised access or modifications) the item in question. 
    Tamper systems can also serve as a warning system to indicate something has been tampered with, like for example medical bottles having a tamper seal on them.
    Chips can have hardware intended for tamper resistance. 

5. What is “roaming”?
    Roaming is when a mobile device connects to a network outside its native network. You will often encounter this when traveling abroad.
    Norwegian networks obviously wont be accessible abroad, so you have to use a network from there, which will route your usage to your provider. 

6. What does the MS acronym stand for?
    Mobile Station. Mobile station refers to the group of all the components you need to communicate with a mobile network.
    A modern mobile phone would be considered a "mobile station", as it features everything you need. 
    Here im assuming you have a valid SIM installed. A phone does not neccesarily come with a SIM of course.

7. What does the PLMN acronym stand for?
    Public Land Mobile Network. PLMN refers to the mobile network owned and ran by an operator. This is the infrastructure your phone connects to.
    From wikipedia:
        A PLMN typically consists of several cellular technologies like GSM/2G, UMTS/3G, LTE/4G, NR/5G, offered by a single operator within a given country, often referred to as a cellular network.
        A PLMN is identified by a globally unique PLMN code, which consists of a MCC (Mobile Country Code) and MNC (Mobile Network Code).
        The IMSI, which usually identifies a SIM, USIM or eSIM for one subscriber, typically starts with the PLMN code. For example, an IMSI belonging to the PLMN 262-33 would look like 262330000000001.

8. What is a “core network” and what does RAN stand for?
    Radio Access Network - the towers and such whos job is to wirelessly connect your phone to the operators network (the core network). 
    The core network is, as the name implies, the core system for the network provider. This is where they handle authentication, routing, usage, etc.

10. Why didn’t one derive session keys in NMT?
    NMT was designed for an era that was very differnt from now. Phones were analog and we didnt have well established security standards (threat models).
    Therefore, the NMT system did not really have much in ways of security. And since it was analog, it couldnt support encryption in this way.

15. What was SS7, and why is it still a security liability?
    Signalling System No. 7.
    From wikipedia:
        A set of telephony signaling protocols developed in the 1970s that is used to setup and teardown telephone calls on most parts of the global public switched telephone network (PSTN). 
            Teardown: A sequence of events used to disconnect a telephone call and return to the ready state for the next call setup. 
    Why still security liability?
        From wikipedia:
            SS7 has been shown to have several security vulnerabilities, allowing location tracking of callers, interception of voice data, intercept two-factor authentication keys, and possibly the delivery of spyware to phones.
                Known about for a long time:
                    In 2008, several SS7 vulnerabilities were published that permitted the tracking of mobile phone users
                    In 2014, the media reported a protocol vulnerability of SS7 by which anyone can track the movements of mobile phone users from virtually anywhere in the world with a success rate of approximately 70%.
            Recent example: 
                In 2026, the Iranian government exploited security vulnerabilities in SS7 to locate US military personnel during the 2026 Iran war.

16. What is the IMSI?
    International Mobile Subscriber Identity number. Used to uniquely identify every user of a mobile network. Kind of like an SSN but for phones.
    The subscriber's permanent identity in the mobile network is such a number.
    IMSI numbers should be kept secret. If you know the number of your target, you can track them using it. 

17. What is a “triplet”?
    Triplet of parameters. The network gives it to the authenticator.
    Has 3 things:
        RAND: Network challenge
        SRES: The expected signed response
        Kc: Cipher key.
    The authenticator sends the challenge to the phone, the SIM computes the challenge. If the challenge is sucessful the SIM is authenticated.
    If success, the Kc is used to encrypt the connection.
