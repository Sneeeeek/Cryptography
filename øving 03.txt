    If a bit is changed in the plaintext, at least 50% of the ciphertext should be different.

4. Is a “fingerprint” and a “digest” the same thing?
    Yes, they both refer to the output of a hash algorithm.

5. What is the preimage and image?
    Preimage is the input, the data before being hashed.
    Image is the output, data after being hashed.

6. What is a collision?
    When two separate things result in the same hash.

7. Are collisions always dangerous? (Why/why not)
    Not neccesarily. Its only dangerous when the hash is being used for security. 

8. What is a 2nd preimage, and why should it be hard to find it?
    A second preimage is a different input that produces the same hash.

12. There are two flavors of iterative hashing – which are they?
    Compression -> Transforms input into a smaller output.
    Permutation -> Transforms input into a same sized output (called sponge function.)

13. Who was Merkle and Damgård?
    Ralph C. Merkle is an american computer scientist and mathematician. He described the Merkle–Damgård construction in 1979. 
    Ivan Bjerre Damgård is a danish cryptographer. 
    The two independently came up with the idea for the construction.

14. What is a “compression function”? (What does it do?)
    A compression function takes in the current has + the next block of data and outputs it to a new hash (fixed size).

15. For a M-D hash, what are the normal block sizes?
    Depends on the implementation, but can be any size.
    MD5, SHA-1, and SHA-256 use 512 bits.
